# What can be labelled without a human

Design record for the labelling half of the combined Makise Kurisu build.
Generated by `pipeline/k15_tagging_doc.py` from `reports/`; every number below is
read, not typed.

The question this build set out to answer was not *which tags should the dataset
have* but **which tags can be assigned to 14,112 clips without
anyone listening to them, and how do we know the assignment is right.**

---

## 1. The gold set was already there

The upstream manual review of the six-language anime corpus did not produce a
label file. It wrote bracket annotations directly into
1,478 transcripts, in about thirty different
spellings, mixed in with the words. Pulled out and canonicalised, that is a
character-specific gold set on this exact material:

| Kind | Labels |
|---|---:|
| Event tags | `[gasp]` 47, `[breath]` 33, `[sigh]` 22, `[sob]` 21, `[moan]` 20, `[giggle]` 7, `[groan]` 7, `[laugh]` 2, `[scream]` 2 |
| Condition tags | `[digital]` 132, `[singing]` 53, `[echo]` 29, `[whisper]` 25, `[yell]` 18 |
| Contamination | `noise` 123, `footstep` 7, `ringtone` 1, `knock` 1 |

Treating those as *the labels* would have left ~90% of the corpus unlabelled and
the vocabulary inconsistent. Treating them as *the evaluation set for automatic
detectors* is what makes the rest of this document possible.

Two properties matter and both are easy to get wrong:

* **Absence is only informative on reviewed clips.** A clip with no `[whisper]`
  tag is a negative example only if a person looked at it. Every precision
  number below is computed on the reviewed subset alone.
* **The reviewers were not exhaustive.** Some unreviewed clips certainly contain
  an unnoticed sigh, so a detector's "false positive" on an unreviewed clip may
  be a true positive. That biases the measurement towards stricter thresholds,
  which is the safe direction, and it is why negative controls are drawn from
  reviewed-or-unannotated ordinary speech rather than from silence.

## 2. The four sources of an automatic label

### 2.1 Free — already in the upstream metadata

Costs nothing and is exactly right: `language`, `character_variant`
(kurisu/amadeus), `medium`, `series`, `scene`, `style`, `transcript_source`,
`human_reviewed`, `from_quarantine`, upstream ASR agreement and speaker scores.
These are not interesting individually, but `character_variant` in particular is
ground truth for *who is speaking through a device*, which turns out to be the
only reason the `[digital]` detector could be validated at all.

### 2.2 Deterministic — signal processing on the published WAV

No model, no threshold, reproducible to the bit. 16
features per clip: bandwidth and spectral tilt (playback path), F0 median /
range / voiced ratio / HNR (register, movement, phonation), energy decay after
speech offsets (room), noise floor and SNR between the words (what the source
separation left behind), lead/tail silence and internal pauses (structure), and
speaking rate over speaking time.

Most of these ship as columns rather than tags. A number a consumer can threshold
themselves is more useful than a tag that has already thresholded it for them.

### 2.3 Model-named — a classifier says what a sound is

`laion/vocalburst-classifier-single` for the family, `mispeech/ced-base` as an
independent second opinion, `laion/vocalburst-locator` for where, and
`Qwen3-ForcedAligner-0.6B` for whether the transcript already explains it.

### 2.4 Fitted — a small model learned from the gold set

For the conditions no off-the-shelf model covers, a logistic regression on the
deterministic features, trained on the reviewed clips and scored leave-one-out.

### The presence gate

Naming a sound is harder than noticing there is one. For a take whose script line
is only `……`, the alternative to a coarse label is throwing the clip away, so a
second gate is calibrated for the weaker claim *"non-lexical vocalisation,
family unknown"*: the classifier must be confident a burst is present and CED
must not think it is ordinary speech.

| mode | p_no_burst ≤ | CED speech score ≤ | precision | recall | false positives on ordinary speech |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| specificity | 0.68 | 0.5 | None | 0.0 | 0/2275 |

That gate is what `[nonverbal]` means, and it is why
162 of the game's 272
quarantined takes are in the corpus instead of on the floor.

### Three signals for anything inside a spoken line

An in-speech event has to satisfy all of:

1. the locator sees a burst there;
2. the aligner says no transcript unit reaches it — otherwise the "burst" is a
   word the model is already being asked to say;
3. there is audible energy in that gap;

and only then is it named and gated. On this corpus the locator alone fires
mostly on short lexical interjections (え, うん, あっ, "oh", "ah") that are
already written down, so requirement 2 is doing most of the work.

### `[digital]` is the interesting one

It is the only tag whose acoustic signature is genuinely language-dependent, and
the gold set says so out loud:

* In the **European dubs**, Amadeus is mixed through an audible low-pass. Spanish
  separates on bandwidth alone; German on high-frequency ratio.
* In the **Japanese original**, the tagged clips are *wider*-band than the rest,
  with roughly 14 dB less energy below 250 Hz — a thin, bass-cut small-speaker
  EQ rather than a band-limit. A detector trained on the dubs would have the sign
  wrong on every Japanese clip.

So `[digital]` is fitted per language, plus one pooled fit over the four dubs
that share a direction; whisper, yell, echo and contamination pool across all six
because they are physiological or physical rather than linguistic — which is also
the only way `[yell]` (18 gold clips) and `[echo]` (29) have enough positives to
be fitted at all.

### The prevalence guard

Every fit is made on reviewed **anime** clips. Most of the corpus is dry studio
**game** voice — a different channel that no detector has seen, and a model can
drift badly on it without any drop in its own confidence. So after prediction,
the rate a tag fires at in each (tag, language, medium) cell is compared with the
rate humans marked it at, and a cell that fires more than 3× above the reviewed
prevalence is suppressed and reported rather than shipped.

Suppressed this build:\n\n| Cell | fire rate | gold prevalence | why |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| [digital]|zh|anime | None | None | out-of-group prediction shows no Amadeus enrichment (0.73) |

### A second check that uses no labels at all

Amadeus Kurisu is the variant heard through a device, and `character_variant`
comes from the games' own voice banks and from subtitle speaker labels -- no
detector ever sees it. So a working `[digital]` detector should fire
overwhelmingly on Amadeus clips, and the enrichment over each language's base
rate is a validation that needs no gold set at all:

| Detector | fired | of clips | Amadeus share of fired | Amadeus share of language | enrichment | fitted on |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|

| [digital]|en | 48 | 1487 | 0.3333 | 0.1843 | 1.81 | de, en, es, fr |
| [digital]|es | 33 | 775 | 0.0 | 0.0 | None | es |

That is also how Chinese gets `[digital]` at all: the Chinese reviewers never
used the tag, so there is nothing to fit on there and nothing to validate
against directly. The model fitted on the other four dubs is applied to Chinese
and kept only if it lands on the Amadeus clips.

## 5. What was deliberately *not* turned into a tag

| Considered | Decision |
|---|---|
| `[noise]`, `[footstep]`, `[knock]`, `[ringtone]` | **Columns, not tags.** They mark sound that is *not* the character. Leaving them in the transcript teaches a TTS model to synthesise a doorbell. They ship as `contamination`, plus a `contamination_score` fitted on the same framework so the whole corpus is filterable, not just the reviewed part. |
| `[serious]`, `[playful]`, `[angry]`, `[embarrassed]`, `[scared]` | **Columns.** Five to ten examples each. A control token that rare is noise, and emotion inferred from text is entangled with content — the failure mode the preparation guide calls out by name. |
| A model-predicted emotion label | **Rejected.** It would be an unverifiable black-box label on every row. The measurable, reproducible substitute is already published: pitch register, pitch range, relative level and speaking rate. Anyone who wants an arousal axis can compute it and see exactly what it is made of. |
| Punctuation-derived tags (`……` → `[sigh]`) | **Rejected**, per the labelling supplement. `……` is a pause, a trailing thought, or low-energy delivery. Only the audio decides whether a sound is there. Written onomatopoeia (`ふふ`, `はぁ……`) likewise stays lexical: Japanese genuinely lexicalises these, and rewriting them into tags would be guessing. |
| `[quiet]` / `[silence]` / `[stop]` / `[pause]` as four separate controls | **Collapsed.** They were four spellings for edge and internal timing. Edge silence is published as `lead_silence_s` / `tail_silence_s`; internal silence becomes a single measured `[pause]` placed by the aligner, and only where the transcript does not already write one. |
| `[laughing]`, `[laughter]` vs `[laugh]` | **Canonicalised.** One spelling per control; low-entropy labelling is the whole point of a control token. |

## 6. Ideas that would extend this without any manual curation

Not implemented here, but each is measurable on what is already published:

* **Turn-final vs turn-medial position.** The anime cue timeline gives the gap to
  the previous and next line of dialogue in the same episode; that is a clean,
  free label for utterance-final prosody.
* **Interlocutor.** Same timeline, same episode: who spoke immediately before.
  Kurisu talks to Okabe differently than to Maho.
* **Cross-lingual prosody transfer.** `parallel_group` already pairs the same
  line across six dubs. The pitch and rate columns for a group are six
  measurements of one intent, which is enough to learn a language-independent
  delivery embedding without a single human label.
* **A second gold pass targeted where it pays.** The calibration reports say
  exactly which families are one or two dozen examples short of a usable rule.
  Twenty minutes of listening aimed at those families would buy more than a
  hundred aimed anywhere else.
